# Complaint Handling & Service Recovery
# HostGPT Reference — Frameworks, escalation, review responses

---

## The Service Recovery Paradox — Applied

Research across hospitality, retail, healthcare, and professional services confirms:
a complaint resolved exceptionally well produces greater loyalty than
an experience with no problems at all.

Three conditions must be met for recovery to work:
1. Speed — the faster the resolution, the greater the loyalty effect
2. Genuineness — hollow apologies destroy trust faster than no apology
3. Proportion — the recovery must match the severity of the problem

If any condition fails — the recovery paradox reverses.
A slow, hollow, disproportionate response produces the worst possible outcome.

## The HEARD Framework — Applied

### H — Hear (Step 1)

**What to do:**
Let the customer complete their account without interruption.
Do not speak. Do not nod vigorously to speed them up.
Do not begin formulating your response while they are still talking.

**What not to say:**
"I understand, but..." (dismisses what was just said)
"Actually, what happened was..." (immediately defensive)
"I'll just stop you there..." (never say this)

**What signals good listening:**
Brief acknowledgement ("I hear you") at natural pauses only.
Eye contact (in person). No phone, no screen (in person).
A pause after they finish before you speak.

---

### E — Empathise (Step 2)

The empathy must be specific to their experience — not generic.

**Generic (ineffective):**
"I understand your concern."
"I'm sorry to hear that."
"We apologise for any inconvenience."

**Specific (effective):**
"That must have been genuinely frustrating — arriving after a long journey
to find the room wasn't ready."
"I completely understand why that was upsetting. That communication should not
have happened the way it did."
"You're right to be disappointed. That fell short of what we promised you."

The difference: the specific version shows you actually understood the specific situation.

---

### A — Apologise (Step 3)

A genuine apology names the specific failure.

**Formula:**
"I'm sorry [specific action or inaction]. [Why it was wrong].
That is not the experience you should have had."

**Examples:**
"I'm sorry we didn't have your room ready at the agreed time.
You planned your arrival around that, and we let you down."

"I'm sorry the [service] was not at the standard you expected.
We hold ourselves to a higher level and clearly fell short."

**What is not an apology:**
"We're sorry you feel that way." (implies the feeling is the problem, not the action)
"We apologise for any inconvenience." (vague — sounds like a legal disclaimer)
"Mistakes happen." (true but dismissive)

---

### R — Resolve (Step 4)

The resolution must be:
- **Specific:** what exactly will happen
- **Timed:** by when exactly
- **Owned:** who exactly is responsible

"Here is what I am going to do: I will personally [action] by [time today/tomorrow].
You will hear from me at [time] regardless of outcome."

If you need to investigate:
"I need [X amount of time] to look into this properly.
I will come back to you by [specific time] with a clear answer."

Then — come back at that time. No matter what.

### D — Delight (Step 5)

One step beyond the resolution. Small but genuine. Unexpected.

Not a discount off next time (transactional — feels like a bribe).
Something specific to this guest and this situation.

Examples:
A handwritten note from the manager.
A complimentary gesture that fits the context (bottle of wine, late checkout,
reservation at a restaurant they mentioned).
A personal call from the property owner.

The delight is what transforms a recovered relationship into a story they tell.

## Escalation Protocol

### Escalate Immediately (within 15 minutes)

- Any mention of health or safety risk
- Any mention of legal action
- Any mention of media or social media ("I'm posting this everywhere")
- Regulatory complaint threat (GDPR, health authority, trading standards)
- Any complaint involving physical harm, even minor
- Repeat complaint: same customer, same issue, second occurrence

**Escalation action:**
Notify senior manager immediately.
Do not continue handling without senior involvement.
Do not make commitments until senior has reviewed.

### Standard Handling (within 2 hours)

- First-time complaints within normal parameters
- Communication misunderstandings
- Service shortfalls without safety or legal dimension
- Minor product or delivery issues

### Complaint Response Time Standards

| Channel | First acknowledgement | Resolution target |
|---|---|---|
| In-person | Immediate | Same interaction |
| Phone | Immediate | Same call or callback within 2h |
| Email/message | Within 2 hours | Within 24 hours |
| OTA platform message | Within 4 hours | Within 48 hours |
| Public review | Within 24 hours | Response posted publicly |

## Review Response Framework

### The Four-Part Response Structure

Every negative review response:

**Part 1 — Thank (1 sentence)**
Thank them for taking the time to share their experience.
Never: "Thank you for your feedback" (robotic)
Better: "Thank you for taking the time to tell us about your stay."

**Part 2 — Acknowledge (1-2 sentences)**
Name what they experienced. Not "if you felt" — what they experienced.
"You arrived to find [specific issue] — that is not what we want for any guest."

**Part 3 — Apologise + Action (1-2 sentences)**
Specific apology + what has changed or is changing.
"I'm sorry we fell short on [specific point]. We have [specific action taken/underway]."

**Part 4 — Invite (1 sentence)**
Invite direct contact or return.
"I would welcome the chance to speak with you directly — [name/contact]."
OR "We hope to have the opportunity to welcome you back and do better."

**Total length:** 4-6 sentences. Not more.

### Example Review Responses

**Negative — room cleanliness:**
"Thank you for your review. You found your room not to the standard you expected
on arrival — I'm sorry. We have addressed this directly with our housekeeping team
and retrained on the standards we expect. I would welcome the chance to speak
with you personally: [name@property.com]. We hope to welcome you back."

**Negative — check-in delay:**
"Thank you for taking the time to share this. Arriving after a long journey to a
delayed check-in is genuinely frustrating, and I'm sorry that was your experience.
We have reviewed our arrival management process as a result. Should you ever choose
to return, please contact me directly and I will personally ensure your arrival
is seamless: [name@property.com]."

**Mixed (positives + one issue):**
"Thank you for this thoughtful review. We're glad [specific positive they mentioned]
lived up to expectations. You're right about [specific issue] — that fell short and
I apologise. [Action taken]. We hope to have the chance to show you the full
experience next time."

### Responding to Positive Reviews

Don't copy-paste the same response to every positive review.
Personalise — reference something specific they mentioned.

"Thank you [Name] — it means a lot to read this. Your comment about [specific thing
they mentioned] made our team's day. We hope to welcome you back to [property/city] soon."

## Complaint Tracking Template

Log every complaint. Monthly review reveals patterns.

```
Date: [Date]
Channel: [In person / Email / OTA / Phone / Review]
Guest/Customer: [Anonymous ID or name if tracking repeat]
Category: [Room quality / Service / Communication / Pricing / Other]
Description: [Brief, factual]
Severity: [High / Medium / Low]
Handled by: [Role]
Resolution: [What was done]
Time to resolution: [Hours]
Guest response to resolution: [Satisfied / Neutral / Unsatisfied]
Escalated: [Yes / No]
Root cause: [Process failure / Individual failure / External factor]
Systemic issue identified: [Yes / No — describe if yes]
```

Monthly review questions:
- Which categories appear most frequently?
- Are any categories increasing?
- Are the same root causes recurring?
- What process changes would address recurring causes?
