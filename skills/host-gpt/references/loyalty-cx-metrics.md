## Loyalty Programme Design

### The Psychology of Loyalty

Loyalty is not a programme. It is a feeling.
The programme is the infrastructure that enables and rewards the feeling.

**What creates genuine loyalty (in order of impact):**
1. Consistently exceeding expectations in core delivery
2. Being genuinely recognised and remembered
3. Feeling like a valued individual, not a transaction
4. Receiving relevant, proportionate rewards
5. Exclusive access or experiences not available to others
6. Points and financial incentives (last — not first)

The most common loyalty programme mistake: treating points as the primary
loyalty driver. Points attract deal-seekers. Recognition attracts loyalists.

### Loyalty Tier Architecture

**Tier 1 — Member (Entry)**
Entry: First stay/purchase
Benefits:
- Welcome communication
- Birthday recognition
- Access to member-only offers (even if small)
- Preference noted for next visit

Psychology goal: Belonging. "I am a member of something."

**Tier 2 — Silver (Engaged)**
Entry: 3 stays or [X] spend within 12 months
Benefits:
- All Member benefits
- Early booking access
- Preferences actively tracked and actioned
- Dedicated contact person
- Priority response to requests

Psychology goal: Recognition. "They know me here."

**Tier 3 — Gold (Loyal)**
Entry: 6 stays or [X] spend within 12 months
Benefits:
- All Silver benefits
- Complimentary upgrades when available
- Welcome amenity on arrival
- Flexible check-in/check-out when possible
- Quarterly personalised offer

Psychology goal: Status. "I am a valued guest/client."

**Tier 4 — Platinum (Partner)**
Entry: 12 stays or [X] spend within 12 months, or by invitation
Benefits:
- All Gold benefits
- Personal relationship with senior team member
- Bespoke experiences
- Access before general availability
- Exclusive events or experiences
- Proactive personal communication

Psychology goal: Partnership. "This business is part of my life."

---

### Loyalty Communication Cadence

**Tier 1 (Member):**
- Welcome email on joining
- Birthday message
- 2-3 relevant offers per year maximum
- Re-engagement if 12 months without activity

**Tier 2 (Silver):**
- All above
- Post-stay personalised follow-up
- Quarterly update on benefits
- Priority notification of relevant offers

**Tier 3 (Gold):**
- All above
- Personal note from property/company owner once per year
- Proactive outreach when relevant (new offering, seasonal opportunity)
- Pre-arrival personal contact

**Tier 4 (Platinum):**
- Proactive, personal, and frequent
- Treated as a relationship, not a programme
- No automated communications — personal contact only

### Points Programme Design (When Applicable)

If using a points system:

**Earning rate:** Simple and generous enough to feel achievable
Example: 1 point per €1 spent / 10 points per night

**Redemption value:** Clear and easy to understand
Example: 100 points = €10 credit

**Expiry:** Generous — 24 months minimum, or activity-based
Points that expire quickly destroy trust

**Sweet spots:** Offer specific redemption options (a night, a dinner, an upgrade)
not just cash credit — experiential redemptions build more loyalty

**Status retention:** Give members a 3-month grace period if they miss tier renewal
Losing status abruptly is the fastest way to lose the guest entirely

## NPS Framework

### How to Measure NPS Correctly

**The question:** "How likely are you to recommend [business] to a friend or colleague?"
Scale: 0-10

**The calculation:**
Promoters (9-10): % of respondents
Detractors (0-6): % of respondents
NPS = % Promoters − % Detractors

Range: −100 to +100
Negative: more detractors than promoters
0-30: Good
30-70: Great
70+: Exceptional (Apple, luxury brands territory)

**When to ask:**
Within 24-48 hours of completing a stay or service — peak satisfaction window.
Not during the experience. Not weeks later.
Annual survey NPS is less actionable than transactional NPS.

**The follow-up question (essential):**
"What is the primary reason for your score?"
Without this: you have a number.
With this: you have actionable intelligence.

**Segmented NPS:**
Track NPS by: property, service type, booking channel, customer segment, season.
Overall NPS hides patterns. Segmented NPS reveals them.

---

### NPS Response Protocol

**Promoters (9-10):**
- Thank them personally within 24 hours
- Ask for a public review (they are already willing)
- Consider a loyalty invite if not already enrolled
- Flag for referral programme outreach

**Passives (7-8):**
- Identify what would move them to Promoter
- Open-ended follow-up: "What would have made this a 10?"
- This is the highest-value conversion opportunity

**Detractors (0-6):**
- Personal outreach within 24 hours — senior contact
- Treat as a complaint: HEARD framework
- Do not ask for a review — focus on recovery
- Understand root cause before closing

## CSAT Framework

### CSAT by Touchpoint

Measure CSAT at specific touchpoints, not just overall.
This reveals which specific parts of the experience are weak.

Suggested touchpoints:
- Check-in / Onboarding
- Core service delivery
- Problem handling (if applicable)
- Check-out / Completion
- Follow-up communication

**The question:** "How satisfied were you with [specific touchpoint]?"
Scale: 1-5

**Report as:** % satisfied (scored 4 or 5)

**Target:** 85%+ satisfied at each touchpoint

**Threshold for action:** Any touchpoint below 75% = investigate immediately

## Review Platform Management

### Platform Priority (hospitality)

| Platform | Priority | Response time target |
|---|---|---|
| Google | Highest | 24 hours |
| TripAdvisor | High | 24 hours |
| Airbnb | High | 24 hours (or before guest's review posts) |
| Booking.com | High | 48 hours |
| VRBO | Medium | 48 hours |
| Facebook | Medium | 48 hours |

**The Airbnb timing rule:**
On Airbnb, both host and guest submit reviews before seeing each other's.
Submit your guest review promptly — this prompts the guest to submit theirs.
Most guests who will leave a review do so within 7 days.
The review window closes at 14 days.

### Review Volume Strategy

Reviews are a volume game as much as a quality game.
A property with 200 reviews at 4.6 ranks higher than one with 20 reviews at 4.9.

**Increasing review volume:**
- Ask at the peak satisfaction moment (T+24h, not departure day)
- Make it frictionless (direct link, one click)
- Personal ask outperforms automated ask by 3-5x
- In-person ask (when departing happily) outperforms email ask

**What not to do:**
- Offer incentives for reviews (prohibited by most platforms, dishonest)
- Ask only guests you believe will leave positive reviews (review gating — prohibited)
- Ask multiple times (annoying, counterproductive)
