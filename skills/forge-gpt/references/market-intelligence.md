## Market Entry Decision Framework

### Stage 1: Market Attractiveness Assessment

**Market size:**
- TAM (Total Addressable Market): Everyone who could theoretically buy
- SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market): Who you can reach with your current model
- SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market): Realistic capture in 12-24 months

**Competition:**
- Who are the established players?
- What is their market share and positioning?
- Where are the gaps or underserved segments?
- What would it cost to compete?

**Regulatory:**
- Any barriers to entry (licences, compliance requirements)?
- GDPR and data protection requirements (EU)?
- Local business entity requirements?
- Import/distribution regulations if applicable?

**Cultural fit:**
- Does your product/service translate culturally?
- Are there language requirements?
- What local adaptations are required?

### Stage 2: Entry Mode Selection

| Entry mode | Speed | Cost | Control | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Digital/remote | Fast | Low | Medium | Low |
| Reseller/distributor | Medium | Low | Low | Medium |
| Partnership | Medium | Medium | Medium | Medium |
| Local hire | Slow | High | High | High |
| Acquisition | Fast | Very high | High | Very high |
| Joint venture | Slow | High | Shared | High |

**Decision rule:**
Start with the lowest-cost, fastest entry mode that can achieve your initial goal.
Upgrade entry mode only when the market has been validated.
Most market entry failures result from over-investing before validation.

### Stage 3: Market Validation Milestones

Before significant investment, validate:
1. **First customer:** Can you close a customer in this market?
2. **Repeatable acquisition:** Can you close a second and third?
3. **Unit economics:** Does the CAC:LTV ratio work in this market?
4. **Retention:** Do customers stay? (Validates product-market fit)
5. **Referral:** Do customers recommend you? (Validates cultural fit)

Only after milestone 3 does significant market investment make sense.

## Cultural Intelligence by Market

### Greek Market (Deep Profile)

**Relationship dynamics:**
Trust is earned slowly and lost quickly. A single negative experience
can end a relationship permanently. Greek business culture values loyalty
and personal relationships over transactional efficiency.

The warm introduction is not merely helpful — it is often required.
A cold approach to a senior decision-maker without a credible introduction
is frequently ignored, regardless of the offer's merit.

**Decision-making:**
Hierarchical. Decisions are often made at the top. Getting access to decision-makers
requires either a strong introduction or building credibility through people they trust.
Consensus at middle management level does not guarantee top-level approval.

**Communication style:**
Direct on substance, indirect on disagreement. Greeks will often not say "no" directly.
Signals of disinterest: "We'll think about it", "We'll be in touch", long silence.
Positive signals: direct follow-up questions, asking for specifics, introducing others.

**Timing:**
Patience is required. Greek business cycles move at their own pace.
Rushing or applying US-style urgency tactics is counterproductive.
The relationship must be solid before the deal can close.

**Meeting protocol:**
Meetings often start late. This is cultural, not disrespectful.
Meals are important — an invitation to lunch or dinner is a positive relationship signal.
Business cards are used and should be treated with respect.

**Language:**
English is widely used in business, especially in Athens and in international contexts.
Any written communication in Greek signals investment in the market and earns respect.
Legal documents should always be in Greek for enforceability.

### German Market (DACH)

**Character:** Formal, process-oriented, risk-averse, thorough.
**Decision-making:** Consensus-based with extensive due diligence. Slow to decide, loyal when committed.
**Communication:** Direct and factual. Claims must be substantiated. Hyperbole is viewed with suspicion.
**Timing:** Punctuality is a form of respect. Late = unprofessional. Meetings start on time and end on time.
**Outreach:** Quality over quantity. A well-researched, specific outreach outperforms volume approaches.
**Sales cycle:** Longer than Anglo-Saxon markets. 3-12 months for significant deals.
**Trust signals:** Certifications, case studies with specific metrics, reference customers in Germany.

---

### French Market

**Character:** Relationship and intellectual engagement valued. Formal initial contact,
warmer once relationship is established.
**Decision-making:** Often centralised. Personal relationships at the right level matter significantly.
**Communication:** French is preferred, especially outside Paris and for formal communication.
English is acceptable in tech/startup sectors.
**Approach:** Lead with ideas and vision, not just ROI. French business culture values intellectual depth.
**Timing:** August is effectively shut down. Christmas/New Year as well.

---

### UK Market

**Character:** More direct than Continental Europe, less direct than US.
Professional scepticism is healthy — claims will be tested.
**Decision-making:** Generally faster than Continental Europe. Risk appetite higher than Germany.
**Communication:** Understated. Over-enthusiasm is viewed with suspicion.
If a Brit says "quite good", they mean excellent. If they say "interesting", they may mean the opposite.
**Approach:** Evidence-based, peer references from UK companies, understated value proposition.

---

### US Market

**Character:** Direct, fast-moving, ROI-focused. High volume, high velocity.
**Decision-making:** Can be fast at SME level. Slower and more political at enterprise.
**Communication:** Direct, benefit-led, metrics-focused. "What's the ROI?" will be asked.
**Approach:** Lead with outcomes, not features. Social proof from recognisable US companies matters.
**Timing:** Fiscal year end (often December or September) creates urgency for budget spend.
Q1 is often high receptivity as new budgets open.

---

### Middle East / GCC

**Character:** Relationship extremely important. Hierarchy matters significantly.
Decisions made at the top. Patience essential.
**Decision-making:** Senior-level. Getting to the right person takes time and introductions.
Local presence or representation is often required for credibility.
**Communication:** Personal meetings preferred over digital for significant decisions.
WhatsApp is widely used for business communication in the region.
**Timing:** Ramadan: significantly slower pace for business. Avoid outreach in this period.
Eid holidays: multiple days off.
**Cultural notes:** Understanding of local business culture and protocols is important.
Local partnerships often required to navigate effectively.

## BD Tool Stack Reference

### Prospecting & Intelligence
- **LinkedIn Sales Navigator:** ICP filtering, trigger event alerts, InMail
- **Apollo.io:** Contact database, sequence automation, analytics
- **Hunter.io:** Email finding and verification
- **Crunchbase:** Funding data, company intelligence, trigger events
- **ZoomInfo:** Enterprise contact data (expensive, high quality)

### Outreach & Sequencing
- **Lemlist:** Email sequences with personalisation, LinkedIn integration
- **Outreach.io:** Enterprise sales engagement platform
- **Salesloft:** Enterprise alternative to Outreach
- **Instantly:** High-volume cold email (warm-up required)
- **Reply.io:** Multi-channel sequences

### CRM
- **HubSpot:** Best for SME/mid-market. Free tier available.
- **Pipedrive:** Simple, visual pipeline. Good for BD-focused teams.
- **Salesforce:** Enterprise standard. Complex, powerful, expensive.
- **Attio:** Modern CRM, relationship-focused. Strong for partnerships.

### Meeting Scheduling
- **Calendly:** Standard. Reduces friction in booking.
- **Chili Piper:** Enterprise routing and scheduling.

### Email Warm-Up (for cold email)
Always warm up new email domains before cold outreach.
Sending cold email from a cold domain will land in spam.
Tools: Lemwarm, Warmup Inbox, Mailreach.
Minimum warm-up period: 4-6 weeks before high-volume sending.
