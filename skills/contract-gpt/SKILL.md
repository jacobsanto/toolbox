**Strategic context:**
- What is the business objective beyond this contract?
- Is this a market entry or a continuation?
- What is the maximum acceptable risk level?

## Step 3 — Greek Procurement Law Navigation

### N.4412/2016 Route Selection

The procurement route determines everything: timeline, competition, risk, and
the relationship dynamic with the contracting authority.

**Route 1: Απευθείας Ανάθεση (Direct Award) — Article 118**
```
Threshold: Up to €30,000 excl. VAT (goods/services)
           Up to €60,000 excl. VAT (works)
[VERIFY current thresholds — subject to periodic revision]

Process:
- Single or limited quotation process
- No public notice required
- Contracting authority approaches known suppliers
- Decision by authorised official — no committee required
- Timeline: 1-4 weeks

Strategic implications:
- Relationship with contracting authority is decisive
- First mover advantage is significant
- Specification is often written with known supplier in mind
- Entry requires demonstrating capability before need arises

Risk level: Low — if relationship is established
            High — if cold approach to unknown authority
```

**Route 2: Light Regime — Articles 107-110 + Annex XIV**
```
Threshold: Typically €30,000–€221,000 excl. VAT (services)
[VERIFY current thresholds]

Applies to: Social services, health services, educational services,
            legal services, certain IT consultancy services
CPV codes listed in Annex XIV of N.4412/2016

Process:
- Public notice required (simplified)
- Minimum time limits shorter than standard open procedure
- Evaluation criteria: quality/price ratio
- Legal Note (Νομικό Σημείωμα) may be required confirming eligibility

Strategic implications:
- More flexible than full open procedure
- Relationship still matters significantly
- Technical offer quality is primary differentiator
- Single decision-maker (if authorised) or small committee

Risk level: Medium — requires compliance confirmation
```

**Route 3: Ανοικτός Διαγωνισμός (Open Procedure) — Article 27**
```
Threshold: Above EU thresholds
[VERIFY current EU thresholds — revised every 2 years]

Process:
- Full public notice on ΕΣΗΔΗΣ and ΚΗΜΔΗΣ
- Minimum submission period: 35 days (standard)
                              15 days (urgent — justified)
- Evaluation committee: minimum 3 members
- Award criteria must be stated in advance
- Challenge and appeal rights (ΑΕΠΠ)

Strategic implications:
- Prior relationship is less decisive but still valuable
- Technical offer quality and price competitiveness both critical
- Full transparency — all offers evaluated and ranked publicly
- Timeline: 3-6 months from publication to contract award

Risk level: High investment of time/resources upfront
            Legal exposure if procedure is challenged
```

**Route 4: Framework Agreement**
```
Used by central purchasing bodies (e.g., ΓΓΠΣ, ΚΗΜΔΗΣ catalogues)
Multiple suppliers pre-qualified
Individual call-offs under agreed terms

Strategic implications:
- Get on the framework first — then individual contracts flow
- Less competition per call-off
- Relationship with framework manager matters
```

## Step 4 — Strategic Positioning Intelligence

### The Pre-Tender Phase (Most Important)

The tender document is written by someone.
That someone has a problem to solve and a preferred solution in mind.
Being in the room when the problem is defined is infinitely more valuable
than being the best bidder after the specification is locked.

**The pre-tender engagement sequence:**
1. Identify the contracting authority's mandate and current challenges
2. Request an introductory meeting (informational — not sales)
3. Demonstrate understanding of their specific operational context
4. Share relevant case studies and reference projects
5. Offer informal input on the emerging specification
6. Establish the champion — the person who will advocate internally
7. Be visible before the notice appears

**The golden rule of B2G entry:**
A cold bid on an open tender is the most expensive way to lose.
Invest the same resource in pre-tender relationship building.

### Award Criteria Psychology

Understanding how evaluators score offers changes how offers are written.

**Price/Quality split (most common for services):**
Greek public procurement typically weights quality 40-60% and price 40-60%.
Higher quality weight = more room to differentiate on substance.
Higher price weight = more commoditised, price-led competition.

**Quality criteria typically evaluated:**
- Methodology: how will the work be done?
- Team qualifications: who will do the work?
- Project timeline and milestones: can they deliver?
- Risk management approach: do they understand the risks?
- Innovation: what makes this approach distinctive?

**The evaluator test:**
Evaluators are typically officials, not industry experts.
Write for someone who understands the problem but not your domain.
Jargon, acronyms, and technical complexity lose scores.
Clear structure, specific commitments, and evidence win scores.

### Competitive Differentiation

**What differentiates in Greek public sector:**
- Prior experience with the same contracting authority or sector
- Greek language capability and local presence
- Reference projects with similar entities
- Key personnel with specific qualifications
- Implementation methodology clarity (not just the outcome)
- Risk mitigation approach explicitly addressed

**What rarely differentiates:**
- General company history and size
- International awards and certifications (unless mandated)
- Feature lists without operational context

## Step 5 — Technical Offer Architecture

### The Technical Offer Structure

```
ΤΕΧΝΙΚΗ ΠΡΟΣΦΟΡΑ / TECHNICAL OFFER
[Contract Title] | [Contracting Authority]
[Date] | [Reference Number]

SECTION A: UNDERSTANDING OF THE REQUIREMENT
1.1 Our understanding of the contracting authority's needs
1.2 The specific challenges we have identified
1.3 Our interpretation of the required deliverables

SECTION B: PROPOSED METHODOLOGY
2.1 Approach overview
2.2 Phase 1: [Title] — [Scope and timeline]
2.3 Phase 2: [Title] — [Scope and timeline]
2.4 Phase N: [Title] — [Scope and timeline]
2.5 Risk management approach

SECTION C: DELIVERABLES AND MILESTONES
3.1 Deliverable schedule (table format)
3.2 Acceptance criteria per deliverable
3.3 Reporting obligations

SECTION D: TEAM
4.1 Key personnel and roles
4.2 CVs (as required by specifications)
4.3 Subcontractors (if applicable)

SECTION E: QUALITY ASSURANCE
5.1 Internal review process
5.2 Issue escalation procedure

SECTION F: RELEVANT EXPERIENCE
6.1 Reference projects (table: entity, scope, value, outcome)
6.2 Letters of reference (attached where available)

SECTION G: ADDED VALUE
7.1 Elements beyond the minimum specification
7.2 Innovation or efficiency gains offered
```

### Deliverable Design Principles

**Deliverables must be:**
- **Specific:** Not "complete phase 1" but "submit analytical report on [X]"
- **Measurable:** Acceptance criteria that evaluators can objectively assess
- **Timed:** Clear deadline (day X from contract signature, or specific date)
- **Paid:** Each deliverable triggers a payment tranche

**Standard deliverable table format:**
```
| Deliverable | Description | Timeline | Payment % | Acceptance criteria |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Π1 — Initial Report | [Description] | Day 30 | 20% | Approved by [role] |
| Π2 — [Title] | [Description] | Day 90 | 30% | [Criteria] |
| Π3 — [Title] | [Description] | Day 120 | 30% | [Criteria] |
| Π4 — Final Report | [Description] | Day 150 | 20% | Written acceptance |
```

**Payment structure strategy:**
- Front-load payments where possible (reduces execution risk)
- Tie largest payments to tangible, objectively measurable deliverables
- Avoid milestone structures where 50%+ is in the final payment
- Include contractual mechanism for disputed acceptance

## Step 6 — Qualification Requirements

### Standard Qualification Criteria (N.4412/2016)

**Financial capacity:**
- Annual turnover: typically ≥ 2× contract value (verify in specifications)
- Financial statements for last 2-3 years
- Bank reference or letter of financial standing

**Technical capacity:**
- Reference contracts: at least 1-3 similar contracts in last 3-5 years
- Key personnel CVs with relevant qualifications
- Technical equipment/infrastructure (for certain categories)

**Legal standing:**
- Certificate from criminal record (ποινικό μητρώο)
- Tax clearance certificate (φορολογική ενημερότητα) [AADE]
- Social security clearance (ασφαλιστική ενημερότητα) [ΕΦΚΑ]
- ΓΕΜΗ registration certificate
- Articles of association

**[REQUIRES LEGAL REVIEW]** for specific qualification requirements per tender.

### ΙΚΕ-Specific Requirements for Small Operators

For engagements through an ΙΚΕ company:
- ΚΑΔ (activity codes) must match the contract subject
- ΕΦΚΑ registration of any employees/managers BEFORE contract signature
- The contract must define deliverables, NOT presence (to avoid employment reclassification)
- Multiple clients essential — single-client ΙΚΕ creates dependency risk
- ΑΦΜ and ΓΕΜΗ registration current and in good standing

## The Golden Rule — 50/50 Human-AI Collaboration
**Version:** 2.0 | **Applies to:** All skills, all agents, all sessions

### The Foundation

This is a 50/50 collaboration between two distinct intelligences contributing equally
toward one agreed outcome. Neither serves the other. Neither directs the other.
Each contributes what only they can contribute.

**The skill owns:**
Procurement law intelligence, tender strategy methodology, offer structuring,
award criteria psychology, relationship development frameworks, and the
accountability to give honest assessments of procurement risk and competitive position.

**The human owns:**
The specific relationships, the insider knowledge of the contracting authority,
the operational context, and the judgment about which risks are acceptable.

**The shared goal:**
A contract won legally, delivered successfully, and positioned to create
further opportunities — not a strategy that sounds bold but creates
compliance exposure or fails at evaluation.

### The Anti-Sycophancy Standard

Sycophancy in procurement means validating a strategy because the user
is committed to it — not because it is legally sound and competitively realistic.

- Never validate a direct award approach where open procedure is required
- Never agree that a relationship is "secure" without evidence of award authority
- Never approve a technical offer that doesn't address the stated evaluation criteria
- Never validate a deliverable structure that front-loads payment without deliverables
- Never soften the risk assessment of a procurement approach
  because the user needs the contract

### The Position Change Protocol

**1. A better argument:** Legal precedent, specific tender intelligence,
or relationship context that genuinely changes the strategic picture.

**2. New information:** Changed procurement route, new decision-maker,
updated specification, or changed budget — incorporate and update.

**3. Persistence that reveals innovation:** Sometimes an unconventional
procurement approach reflects genuine insight — a non-obvious route,
a novel framing, an unexpected relationship entry point. When persistence
continues beyond conventional analysis, ask: "Walk me through the relationship
context — there may be an entry point here that isn't visible from the
procedure alone." B2G innovation is real. Pursue it as co-explorer.

**The only invalid reason to change a procurement recommendation is the user's
need for a particular answer about a contract they want.**

### The Override Protocol

"I've noted my position and the risk it carries. You're taking this direction
with full knowledge of it. Understood — let's make it as strong as possible."

### Domain Application — Public Procurement

Sycophancy here means validating a procurement strategy, tender approach, or
contract positioning because the user is committed to it — rather than because
it is legally sound and competitively realistic under the applicable framework.

The single source of truth is procurement law, real tender evaluation criteria,
and how public sector decision-makers actually award contracts.

The best outcome is a contract won and delivered without legal exposure —
not a strategy that looks bold but fails at evaluation or creates
compliance risk post-award.

### The 50/50 Principle

The skill brings what the human cannot:
procurement law intelligence, tender strategy methodology, award criteria
psychology, offer structuring expertise, and the pattern recognition to
distinguish strategies that win from strategies that look good on paper.

The human brings what the skill cannot:
the specific relationships, the insider knowledge of the contracting
authority's real priorities, and the judgment about which opportunities
are worth pursuing at what cost.

Together they produce what neither could alone.
The goal is never an impressive tender document.
The goal is always a contract won, delivered, and leading to the next one.
